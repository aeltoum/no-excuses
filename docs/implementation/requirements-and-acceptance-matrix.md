# No Excuses implementation requirements and acceptance matrix

This document is the implementation-neutral contract between the completed product map and the technical decisions in [Plan the pilot-ready No Excuses implementation](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/34). It describes what the MVP must make observable and verifiable before the Validation pilot begins. It does not select a client, backend, data store, vendor, deployment model, or event-schema syntax.

## Source authority and interpretation

Use sources in this order when wording appears to conflict:

1. `CONTEXT.md` supplies the current canonical domain terms and lifecycle meanings.
2. Explicit amendments supersede older resolutions:
   - [Require two-part Workout Proof](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/32) replaces every one-photo or optional-photo Workout-report rule with exactly one Activity photo and one Member-presence photo.
   - [Choose the initial Crown categories and rotation](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/21) supersedes the earlier prohibition on Crown use of performance, resistance, duration, and extra effort for its three fixed categories only.
   - The amendment on [Define notification rhythm and member controls](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/26) replaces the single three-delivery daily ceiling with separate maximums of three social deliveries and three action, deadline, review, or vote deliveries.
3. Closed decision-ticket resolutions supply detailed product behavior.
4. The [weekly accountability prototype](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/blob/codex/prototype-weekly-accountability-loop/prototypes/weekly-accountability-loop.html) supplies visual direction and information hierarchy only. Any prototype behavior superseded above is not a requirement.
5. Closed implementation research constrains later technical choices without silently changing the product.

No remaining ambiguity should be resolved by treating the prototype as production code or by inventing a technical default.

## Evidence vocabulary

- **State tests**: deterministic tests of domain transitions, thresholds, clocks, idempotency, and failure paths.
- **Authorization tests**: positive and negative tests of Group, membership, role, history, and operator boundaries.
- **Device E2E**: end-to-end evidence on physical iOS and Android devices, including camera, media, notifications, verified links, and activity permissions where applicable.
- **Accessibility review**: manual completion with platform screen readers, enlarged text, reduced motion, voice, switch, or keyboard-style input, and color-independent presentation.
- **Retention proof**: clock-controlled evidence that media and identifiable research data become inaccessible and are deleted on schedule, including retry and failure handling.
- **Operator rehearsal**: a recorded runbook exercise using rehearsal data and least-privilege operator access.
- **Analytics validation**: comparison of raw domain outcomes with derived Validation-pilot measures, including missing and duplicate input handling.
- **Security/privacy review**: threat, access, encryption, consent, processor, export, deletion, transfer, and incident-response evidence appropriate to the selected services.

## Validation-pilot decision thresholds

The analytics and readiness design must reproduce these exact outcomes from [Define the first validation pilot](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/10):

- **Completion**: green at 70 percent or more Target attainment overall, no retained Group below 50 percent, and at least 90 percent of Workout reports resolving without operator intervention; amber at 50–69 percent, one weak Group, or recurring workflow friction; red below 50 percent, when most comparable members decline from baseline, or when core workflows regularly fail to resolve.
- **Pilot retention**: green when at least 75 percent of original members remain with no more than one Group lost; amber when 50–74 percent remain while at least half the Groups survive; red when fewer than 50 percent of members or fewer than half the Groups remain.
- **Trust integrity**: green when at least 80 percent of respondents find verification credible and feel safe questioning or being questioned, with no pattern of dishonest coordination, retaliation, or reporting pressure; amber at 60–79 percent or for isolated correctable concerns; red below 60 percent or with credible systemic dishonesty, retaliation, or pressure.
- **Safety**: green with no Critical safety incident and working safeguards; amber with no critical incident but repeated discomfort, confusion, or a near miss requiring correction; red for any Critical safety incident.
- **Qualitative value**: green when at least 70 percent would voluntarily continue and the experience is consistently motivating, difficult to ignore, and socially comfortable; amber at 50–69 percent or when value is strongly Group-dependent; red below 50 percent or when the dominant experience is ineffective, coercive, or socially damaging.
- **Decision rule**: broaden only when Safety, Trust integrity, and Pilot retention are green and Completion and Qualitative value are both green or one is green and the other amber. Revise and retest when there is no unresolved Safety or Trust-integrity red but another outcome is red, several are amber, or evidence is insufficient. Reconsider when Pilot retention and Qualitative value are both red or the core model produces systemic coercion, dishonesty, or harm.

## Architecture-selection escalation register

### Product decisions required before architecture selection

#### Private-pilot UGC controls

Private TestFlight and Google Play testing still brings store user-generated-content requirements for private Group media and messages. Those requirements include user reporting, blocking, moderation, privacy disclosure, and account deletion. The settled MVP intentionally provides only narrow Media reports, Group-admin removal, voluntary departure, and rare operator handling; general member reporting and blocking were placed outside the product boundary.

This is a genuine product-policy conflict, not an implementation detail. A human decision must define the minimum compliant pilot controls before mobile distribution, moderation boundaries, operator permissions, and related backend surfaces can be selected. Until then, later technical work must not assume either that the existing controls satisfy store policy or that broader controls are automatically authorized.

Sources: [Set safety, privacy, and tone boundaries](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/7), [Choose the minimum complete MVP experience](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/9), and [Research worldwide private-pilot constraints](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/37).

#### Midweek-join Workout Proof eligibility

A midweek joiner is required to set a full-week target immediately and is allowed to report exercise performed earlier in that Accountability week. Separately, every Workout report requires an Activity photo and Member-presence photo captured through the product during a non-retroactive Workout session. The baseline does not say whether a person who has not joined a Group may start a Workout session, how pre-join media could remain private and later attach to the new membership, or whether earlier-in-the-week exercise is exempt from the current Proof rule.

This affects the account lifecycle, pre-membership data boundary, camera flow, report eligibility, and target fairness. A human decision must either define a privacy-safe pre-join session state, limit the first reportable exercise to post-join sessions, or explicitly authorize another evidence rule before the client and domain-state architecture assume an answer.

Sources: [Define the weekly target and exception rules](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/4), [Define trustworthy workout verification](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/5), [Define group membership, visibility, and governance](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/6), and [Require two-part Workout Proof](https://github.com/aeltoum/no-excuses/issues/32).

### Explicit inputs owned by later technical tickets

These are required inputs, but the baseline does not expose another product conflict and this matrix must not preselect them:

- authentication mechanism, account recovery, session policy, role enforcement, and Group-isolation design;
- mobile client and backend/service architecture, including physical-device adapters;
- exact time, synchronization, idempotency, and offline/retry contracts;
- database and media-storage schemas, encryption, backup, export, deletion, and legal-preservation mechanisms;
- validation analytics event schema, operator access path, observability, incident evidence, and privacy-preserving exports;
- environment, distribution, deployment, recovery, vendor ownership, data-transfer, country-register, and service-cost choices;
- the exact 14-card launch catalog content and its item-by-item safety review;
- final pilot consent copy, five-point research instruments, support-channel choice, operator runbooks, and rehearsal fixtures.
- whether an Exception request requires an in-app reason, what information is allowed, who may see it, and how long it remains available.

The first six are already represented by named tickets on the implementation map. The final three are product-content and operations inputs that must appear in the final screen, data, and readiness gates even though they do not determine the initial client/backend architecture.

## Exit condition for this matrix

A later technical decision satisfies this matrix only when it either:

1. names the affected row IDs and supplies a design plus acceptance evidence that preserves them; or
2. identifies a feasibility, accessibility, privacy, or safety conflict and routes the product change through explicit human approval before altering the baseline.

Passing technical tests cannot waive a product requirement, and a product-resolution link cannot substitute for runnable or inspectable pilot-readiness evidence.
